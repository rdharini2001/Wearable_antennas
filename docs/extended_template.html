03 / Methodology

Deterministic prediction and frequency-marginal uncertainty

I · Learning

Continuous ACO–SVR

RBF-SVR parameters are selected by ant-colony exploration using only training-fold validation. Penalty and width use logarithmic, data-relative search spaces; the insensitive region uses training-sweep curvature.

K(x,z) = exp(−‖x−z‖² / 2σ²)

Reference: Socha & Dorigo (2008); Smola & Schölkopf (2004).

II · Reconstruction

Frequency-domain correction

For represented antenna designs, a local cubic interpolant reconstructs residual frequency variation left by the base regressor. Tree models use direct spectral interpolation because discontinuous predictions create irregular residuals.

ŷ(g,f) = fSVR(g,f) + sg(f)

The spline family is chosen by a separate training-only validation subset; test targets never enter interpolation.

III · Frequency ablation

Geometry-only prediction

Without frequency, the model cannot distinguish different points in the same antenna sweep. A conditional response distribution is learned from training sweeps. Its mean and median yield distinct point forecasts, while calibrated prediction intervals express uncertainty when frequency is unknown.

ŷ(g) ≈ E[Y | geometry = g]

CRPS and interval coverage evaluate distribution quality; these are not substitutes for pointwise MSE and MAE.

Additional evaluation: antenna-mean prediction

A geometry-level score compares each design’s predicted average signal strength with its observed average over held-out frequencies. These design-average errors characterize a different target and cannot replace the paper’s pointwise error figures.

04 / Frequency-marginal inference

Uncertainty when operating frequency is unavailable

A single geometry corresponds to an entire frequency-response sweep. Rather than invent an unobserved operating frequency, the extended model predicts an empirical distribution of signal strength. A disjoint calibration subset sets the width of its 90% predictive interval.

Conditional-response model

p(y | g) = distribution of training responses at geometry g

A point estimate cannot recover the response at an omitted frequency. The conditional distribution represents the remaining frequency-dependent variation. Its quality is assessed using continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), predictive interval coverage, and interval width.

Statistical grounding: Gneiting & Raftery (2007), proper scoring rules; Romano, Patterson & Candès (2019), split-conformal uncertainty quantification.

Interpretation

Pointwise geometry-only error has a measurable floor because distinct test responses share identical inputs. The test-set oracle lower bound is — RMSE, even when its geometry means are chosen with access to the evaluation data. This is a diagnostic bound, never a training parameter.

For this fixed test set, the seven unmet pointwise thresholds are beyond these geometry-only oracle bounds. The extended distributional model captures uncertainty rather than silently changing the target. Exact experimental replication is a distinct claim.
